## 1. Posting times

### 1a. The owner's times converted (from 2 Nov 2026)

US clocks went back at 2:00 a.m. on **Sunday 1 Nov 2026**, so Monday 2 Nov was the first full weekday on EST. Lisbon has been on WET (UTC+0) since 25 Oct. The gap is now **5 hours to New York and 8 hours to Los Angeles**.

| Lisbon | New York (ET) | Chicago (CT) | Los Angeles (PT) | What US viewers are doing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 12:00 | 7:00 AM | 6:00 AM | 4:00 AM | East: waking up or commuting. West: asleep |
| **18:00** | **1:00 PM** | 12:00 PM | **10:00 AM** | East: lunch scroll. West: mid-morning |
| 20:00 | 3:00 PM | 2:00 PM | 12:00 PM | East: afternoon. West: lunch |
| **22:00** | **5:00 PM** | 4:00 PM | **2:00 PM** | East: end of the workday. West: afternoon |
| 00:30 | 7:30 PM | 6:30 PM | 4:30 PM | East: evening on the couch |

**Changes coming up:**
- **14–27 Mar 2027:** the US moves its clocks first, so the gap drops to 4 h for two weeks. Post one hour earlier in Lisbon (17:00 and 21:00) to keep the same US times.
- **From 28 Mar 2027:** Europe moves too, and the gap is back to 5 h.
- **Simplest fix:** set the scheduler to US Eastern time and never convert by hand.

### 1b. What the data says about US posting times

| Source | Sample | Best for a US audience (audience local time) | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sprout Social, Instagram 2026 ([link](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/best-times-to-post-on-instagram/)) | ~2B engagements, 307K profiles, Nov 2025–Feb 2026 | Mon 2–4 PM, Tue 1–7 PM, Wed 12–9 PM, Thu 12–2 PM. Weekends weakest | Medium (engagement on all post types, not Reels views) |
| Adobe Express, Reels 2026 ([summary](https://ppc.land/instagram-reels-posted-at-3-a-m-gain-2-1-times-the-views-of-9-p-m-posts/)) | 20K Reels from 149 top creators, April 2026, ET | **Noon ET** 2nd best for views. **4–11 PM ET underperformed** | Low (large accounts, global audiences, thin cells) |
| PostFast, Reels ([link](https://postfa.st/blog/best-time-to-post-reels-on-instagram)) | 5.2K Reels, 158 workspaces | Weekdays **~1–3 PM ET** had the highest median reach. Sat ~10 AM ET beat accounts' own averages | Low (small, vendor) |
| Buffer, TikTok 2026 ([link](https://buffer.com/resources/best-time-to-post-on-tiktok)) | 7.1M posts | Evenings 6–11 PM strongest overall, afternoons weakest. Sat is the best day; peaks such as Mon 1 PM and Thu 1 PM | Medium (big sample, but engagement rate only, time zone unclear) |
| SocialPilot, YouTube incl. Shorts ([link](https://www.socialpilot.co/insights/best-time-to-post-on-youtube)) | 301K videos | Shorts: **12–3 PM** and **7–9 PM** local. Fri 4–7 PM strongest. 3–7 AM worst | Low (not split by format, internally inconsistent) |

**How to read this:**
- **Where the studies agree:** a US early-afternoon window (about 12–3 PM ET) shows up for Instagram, Shorts and some TikTok days.
- **Where they disagree:** the evening (TikTok and Shorts say good, Instagram studies say weaker).
- **Time of day matters less than the hook.** Reels and Shorts keep getting distributed for days (GAME_PLAN: old reels get second waves). Posting time mostly decides who sees the post in its **first hours**, which is the test audience the platform learns from.

- At 7 AM ET it's 4 AM on the West Coast.
- Only one of the five studies (Buffer TikTok, a few weekday mornings) points there.
- Its first-hour viewers would be mostly Europeans, who are not the target audience.

**The trade-off in one line:** later posts reach more awake Americans; earlier posts are easier for the owner to manage. 18:00 + 22:00 is the best balance. If the owner wants a daytime routine, keep 12:00 **only for Instagram trial reels**. Those go to non-followers anyway, though there is no data showing timing matters less for trials.

- **Then compare** the median slot-B views and skip rate between the two periods.

**Scheduling:**

### 2b. How to post a series

**Recommendation: one episode a day, on consecutive days, at the same time (slot A, 18:00 Lisbon).**

- **Each episode gets its own first-hours test.** Two episodes a few hours apart compete for the same followers' attention and blur which episode worked. (This is reasoning, not a measured study.)
- **A daily "come back tomorrow" is a follow reason.** Series are what turn viewers into followers:
  - The page in our reference notes gained 27K followers from 13 posts with a "DAY 1/7" series (LEARNINGS).
  - Vendor figure: series convert about 67% better than standalone Shorts *(low)*.
- **A six-episode run gives six follow moments** instead of three days of double posts.
- **Production matches it:** about one build a day means one episode a day.
- **Data stays clean:** one episode per day gives one row per episode in the tracker.
- **Other creators' advice on timing has no hard data behind it.** No large study compares same-day and next-day release, so treat this as a reasoned default and check it with the tracker.

**Rules for the series:**
1. **Bank rule:** never post Episode N unless Episode N+1 is already rendered and checked. A broken "follow for Ep 2" promise costs trust.
2. **Breakout rule:** if an episode reaches **3× our median views within ~6 h**, or the comments ask "part 2?", post the next (banked) episode early in **slot B the same day**. The rest of the series keeps its daily rhythm from there.
3. **Each episode works alone:** a 2-s recap line ("Last time: …") plus the "EP 3/6" badge. Most viewers will meet the series in the middle.
4. **Link the parts together:**
   - Pinned comment "Ep 2 is up 👉" on the previous episode.
   - Instagram's Reels series linking (rolled out from Aug 2025) where the account has it.
   - TikTok playlist, if the account has the option.
   - YouTube playlist plus the "Related video" link (see section 3).
5. **Episode order:** **put the strongest "you" hook first.** Ep1 is the entry point that gets pinned and linked. For example, "why your money is worth less every year" hooks harder than "how money started". Each episode stands alone anyway, so a non-chronological order is fine. The calendar follows the order the scripts are numbered (money1 → money6). Reorder before posting if the writers agree.
6. **If the series ends up with only 5 episodes:** slot A on Wed 11 Nov takes a backlog video (Sink or Elevator) instead.

## 4. Testing framework

### 4a. What to measure on every post (read at 24 h and at 7 days)

| Metric | Where to find it | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| **Hook:** IG **skip rate** (% who left in the first 3 s); YT **stayed to watch** (viewed vs swiped away); TT % still watching at 0:02–0:03 on the retention curve | IG Reel insights (skip rate replaced view rate in Aug 2025, [Social Samosa](https://www.socialsamosa.com/news-2/instagram-retention-chart-skip-rate-new-performance-metrics-reels-9730992)); YT Studio → Analytics → Audience retention; TT Analytics → video → retention | Is the first frame and first line working? |
| **Average % watched** = average watch time ÷ length | All three | Is the body holding people? |
| **Shares per 1,000 reach** = shares (sends) ÷ reach × 1,000 | IG (reach, shares); TT (shares ÷ views); YT (shares ÷ views) | Instagram's strongest signal for non-follower reach |
| **Saves per 1,000 reach** | IG, TT | Practical value |
| **Follows per 1,000 views** | IG (follows), TT (new followers from the video), YT (subscribers gained) | Does the video convert viewers into followers? |
| Non-follower % of reach | IG | Did it break out of the follower base? |
| Traffic source | TT (For You vs Search), YT (Shorts feed) | Is search working? |

### 4b. Tracker (copy into a sheet; one row per post per platform)

| Date | Slot | Platform | Video / variant | Type (main / trial / cut / carousel) | Length (s) | Views 24 h | Views 7 d | Reach | Skip % (IG) / Stayed % (YT) / 3 s hold % (TT) | Avg watch (s) | Avg % watched | Shares | Shares /1k reach | Saves | Saves /1k | Follows | Follows /1k views | Non-follower % | Notes (hook type, what changed) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 04-11 | A | IG | airplanemode | main | 60 | | | | | | | | | | | | | | question hook |
| 04-11 | B | IG | airplanemode_30_q | trial | 30 | | | | | | | | | | | | | | T1a |

**Every Sunday:**
- Compute each platform's **median of the last 10–20 main posts** for each metric (trials get their own median).
- Those medians are the benchmarks; no platform publishes official thresholds.
- Log one line per week in LEARNINGS.

**Our starting point:** bridge (TikTok) kept only ~40% of viewers at 0:02, watched 27% on average, and gained 0 follows.

**Early targets until our own medians exist:**
- YT stayed to watch **≥ 70%**; under 60% rarely does well (Galloway, 3.3B views).
- IG skip rate falling below **50%** (one strategist aims for about 20% *(low)*).
- Average % watched **≥ 50%** on 30–45 s videos.

### 4c. Decision rules

1. **Wait before judging:** don't judge before 48 h, and give the final verdict at 7 days (old reels get second waves).
2. **Make a part 2 when:** at 7 days the video has **≥ 2× median views**, AND either shares or saves per 1,000 is ≥ 1.5× median, or ≥ 5 comments ask for more. Post the part 2 within 3–7 days with the same cover template and a "PART 2" badge, and pin a link to it on part 1.
3. **Re-cut a winner when:** it's in the top 20% by shares per 1,000. Make a 30-s cut with a new hook and post it as a trial reel; if the trial beats the trial median, share it to everyone.
4. **Hook problem or body problem?**
   - **Bad hook metric, decent % watched:** change only the first frame and first line, then re-test as a trial.
   - **Good hook, % watched below median:** cut it down (30-s version) and add a re-hook earlier.
5. **Drop or rework a format** (a topic family, length or hook type) after **3 posts in a row below median on BOTH the hook metric and shares per 1,000**. One bad post means nothing; hits are spiky (COMPETITOR_SCAN).
6. **Is the series working?** Compare follows per 1,000 on series episodes with single videos. If it isn't ≥ 1.5× by the end of the money series, rethink the series format before Money Season 2. Episode-to-episode view drop-off is normal; follows are what count.
7. **Trial reels:** change one variable, compare only with other trials, and believe a pattern only when **2 tests agree**. Single trial results are noise ([Planoly](https://www.planoly.com/blog/do-trial-reels-work-research)).
8. **Posting frequency:** 2 a day is supported by data, as long as quality holds:
   - Buffer's within-account analysis found Instagram reach per post **rises** with frequency (10+ posts a week ≈ +24% vs 1–2) ([Buffer IG](https://buffer.com/resources/how-often-to-post-on-instagram/)).
   - On TikTok, the median views per post stayed flat while breakout chances rose ([Buffer TT](https://buffer.com/resources/how-often-should-you-post-on-tiktok/)).
   - **If slot-A medians fall for 2 weeks in a row, cut slot B first,** never the quality of slot A.
