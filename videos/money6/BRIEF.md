**Twist I considered and rejected:** "Invest only from 25 to 35, then stop, and you beat starting at 35." At 7% that gives $237,631 vs $213,425, which is true. But at 6% it **flips** ($170,303 vs $177,849). Because it depends on the assumption, it's not robust enough to state as fact.

## Angle
- **Hook:** the proven "habit vs investing" money test.
- **Payoff:** over $400,000 from coffee money.
- **Twist:** start 10 years later and you get half. "The coffee was never the problem. Time was." This flips the usual "stop buying lattes" lecture, which viewers resent, into a principle about time.
- **Series link:** the line "after inflation" builds on EP3. "Interest works for him" pays off EP5's last line.
- **Closing vote:** "Did you start investing before 30?"

## Who would someone send this to?
**The friend who says "I'll start investing later"**, or **a younger sibling or kid just starting their first job**. Also a parent sending it to their 22-year-old.

## Why it should perform
- **The exact format the game plan picked** for the first money test: a 25-vs-35 comparison with racing counters (game plan §3b, from finance Reels research). Finance formats that work: side-by-side comparisons and myth-busting.
- **A myth, then truth flip** ("cut the coffee" → "it's time, not coffee") avoids the preachy tone that gets scrolled past (rule 18).
- **Big, concrete, racing numbers:** over $400,000 vs $88,000 put in; half if you wait (rule 17; viral refs rule 3: something on screen changes every second).
- **A decision-question vote** to close (rule 23; viral refs rule 22).
- **No luxury flexing and no stock picks** (rule 28; game plan §3b).

## Script (99 words, ~33 s)
1. Jake spends 6 dollars a day on coffee. Here's what it really costs.
2. About 180 dollars a month. But say he invests it instead, from age 25.
3. Assume 7 percent a year after inflation, near the American stock market's long-run average. Not guaranteed, not advice.
4. By 65: over 400,000 dollars. He only put in about 88,000.
5. Episode 6 of 6. Follow for season 2.
6. But start at 35 instead, and he ends with about 200,000. Half.
7. Ten years of waiting cost him over 200,000.
8. So the coffee was never the problem. Time was. Did you start investing before 30?
