## Angle
- **Story:** Jake finds Grandma's birthday card from 2000 with a $100 bill inside.
- **Payoff first:** it now buys half.
- **Myth, then truth:** "the Fed wants zero inflation" → no, it wants 2% on purpose.
- **Stakes now:** prices up 3.7% this year, doubling in ~19 years at that pace.
- **Hook into EP4:** "Is your savings beating 3.7 percent?"

## Who would someone send this to?
**Mom or Dad, or the grandparent who still gives $100 in a card**, and **the friend who keeps their savings in cash or in a 0% checking account**. Inflation is the most universal money pain right now, so the "practical value" share is strong.

## Why it should perform
- **A "you" question with stakes in the first line,** with the search phrase spoken and shown ("worth less every year") (rules 2 and 9; growth playbook: TikTok reads spoken and on-screen keywords).
- **A specific, verifiable number turned into an image:** a grocery cart where half the items vanish (rule 17).
- **Contrarian twist:** "the Fed wants inflation, on purpose" (rule 18).
- **Comes back to mind daily:** every grocery receipt (STEPPS "triggers").
- **Everyday money A-vs-B** (2000 vs 2026), a top performer on the reference pages (viral refs rules 7 and 10).

## Script (100 words, ~33 s)
1. Why is your money worth less every year? Jake's 100 dollars from 2000 now buys half as much.
2. Whatever it bought back then now costs about 194 dollars.
3. That's inflation: prices creep up, so each dollar buys less.
4. You'd think the Fed wants zero inflation. But it aims for 2 percent a year. On purpose.
5. It says steady, low inflation helps people plan.
6. Episode 3 of 6. Follow along.
7. But in the last year, prices rose 3.7 percent. At that pace, they double in 19 years.
8. So money sitting in the bank quietly shrinks. Is your savings beating 3.7 percent?
