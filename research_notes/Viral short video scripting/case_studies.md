# Case Studies: Viral Faceless / Animated Explainer and Cartoon Story Pages (Shorts, Reels, TikTok) and Their Scripting Patterns

Research date: 2026-11-05. All view and subscriber counts are from third-party trackers or press unless noted, and are dated where possible. Fan wikis, vendor blogs and "AI tool" marketing pages are weak sources and are flagged as such.

## Zack D. Films: format, hooks, length, pace, how visuals carry the explanation, endings, growth, interviews

### Takeaway
Zack D. Films is the clearest proof of scale for the format. It went from about 19.3M subs and 29.4B views (Feb 2025) to about 29.6M subs and 82.3B views (Nov 2026), and was adding about 3.7B views per 28 days in late 2026. The scripting pattern is one plain-language "what if / how does X work" question, then a common-sense rule, then a "But" twist, then a "So" consequence, narrated calmly over literal close-up 3D visuals. I found no first-party interview about his process.

### Cited Findings
- **Growth (Feb 2025):** Tubefilter's US top-50 chart for the week of 02/09/2025 listed Zack D. Films at about 19,300,000 subscribers and 29,393,345,225 all-time views. — [Tubefilter, Feb 2025](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/02/10/top-50-most-viewed-us-youtube-channels-week-of-02-09-2025/)
- **Growth (Oct 2026):** Tubefilter's worldwide chart for the week of 10/11/2026 listed about 29,000,000 subscribers and 79,318,500,885 all-time views. — [Tubefilter, Oct 2026](https://www.tubefilter.com/?p=194612)
- **Growth (Nov 2026):** vidIQ, as of 2026-11-05, showed 29.6M subscribers, 82,307,648,726 total views and 4,175 videos. The channel joined June 7, 2021. Its last 28 days added +700K subs and 3.68B views. Monthly totals were October 2026 +900K subs / 4.43B views, September +300K / 2.08B, and August +400K / 2.45B. Daily view gains ranged from about 42M to 320M. — [vidIQ stats](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/%40zackdfilms/)
- **Video count implies very high cadence:** 4,175 videos since June 2021 averages about 2 uploads per day. That is my arithmetic from vidIQ's numbers. — [vidIQ stats](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/%40zackdfilms/)
- **Fan-wiki cadence claim:** A fan wiki says the channel "currently uploads 4-5 shorts a day" and also makes longer videos. The claim is undated and unverified. — [Zack D. Films Fandom wiki](https://zackdfilms.fandom.com/wiki/Zack_D._Films)
- **Recent title example:** The latest video, "How To Tell If You're Dreaming 😴" (Nov 3, 2026), had 3.89M views after about 1 day 5 hours. Titles take the form of a plain question or "how to" phrase plus one emoji. — [vidIQ stats](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/%40zackdfilms/)
- **vidIQ's "top videos" list looks like long-form only.** Examples are "Ohio's 1986 Balloon Disaster" (20.34M), "The Pepsi Contest That Killed 5 People" (12.53M), "Toyota's $2,300,000,000 Mistake" (10.63M) and the compilation "13 Unreleased Videos I Thought Would Go Viral" (May 2026, 11.46M). Individual Shorts are probably not in that list, so it says little about Shorts reach per video. — [vidIQ stats](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/%40zackdfilms/)
- **Trackers conflict on format mix.** OutlierKit estimates about 100% long-form recent uploads averaging about 7:24, and Vidanalyze found no public Shorts in its cached data. This conflicts with the channel's known Shorts output and suggests tracker artifacts. Treat tracker format splits as unreliable. — [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/channel/zackdfilms); [Vidanalyze](https://vidanalyze.com/channel/zackdfilms/shorts)
- **Shift to CGI:** Early videos used real photos and footage with Zack on camera. From mid-2023 he moved toward CGI/Blender, and by late 2023 videos were fully CGI. — [NamuWiki](https://en.namu.wiki/w/Zack%20D.%20Films)
- **Narration notes from a fan wiki:** He records with a microphone, and his voice is "deeper in certain videos depending on the topic." Wiki-level evidence only. — [Sportskeeda Wiki](https://wiki.sportskeeda.com/youtube/who-is-zack-d-films)
- **Branding inside the visuals:** Sources note that phrases like "ZackD" or Zack's face are placed on objects in the video, so the brand lives in the visuals and not in an intro. — [Guidesify](https://opinion.guidesify.com/zack-d-films-the-man-who-explains-various-random-interesting-topics-across-the-internet/)
- **Example script (sun video, fan transcript):** "If you poured a giant bucket of water onto the sun... Well, water puts out normal fires because it cools the fuel and helps block oxygen. But the sun isn't a normal fire... So, if you flooded it with an enormous amount of water, you'd be giving it more hydrogen to fuse." — [Fandom transcript, via search snippet](https://zackdfilms.fandom.com/wiki/13_Unreleased_Videos_I_Thought_Would_Go_Viral/Transcript). The page itself returned HTTP 402 when fetched, so only the snippet was seen.
- **Third-party "style recipe" (commercial, unaffiliated AI tool page):** One surprising question up front, glossy 3D visuals, extreme close-ups, a calm narrator, and an ending that asks the viewer something. Topics cover the human body, strange true stories and "what would happen if" questions. — [Ziztu Reel Factory](https://getziztu.com/reel-factory/zack-d-films-style)

### Inferences
- **Script skeleton (inferred from the transcript):** Hypothetical question as the hook, then "Well," plus the intuitive rule, then "But" plus the exception, then "So" plus the surprising consequence. This is the same "but/so" causal chain Jenny Hoyos teaches (see the creator-scripting section), applied to science instead of stunts.
- **Visuals carry the explanation literally.** Each sentence appears to map to one concrete visual (bucket, sun, fire, hydrogen), so the narration works as captions for a sequence of literal shots. This matches Hoyos's "understandable on mute" rule. It is an inference from the descriptions, not a frame-by-frame analysis.
- **Calm, conversational, second-person narration** ("if you poured...", "you'd be giving it...") puts the viewer inside the hypothetical. Topic-matched vocal tone (deeper for darker topics) is the main dramatic device instead of music swells. Anecdotal.
- **Volume is part of the strategy.** About 2+ uploads per day on average across 5+ years, plus long-form compilations that recycle Short ideas ("13 Unreleased Videos..."), suggests a high-volume idea-testing machine.

### Gaps
- I found no first-party interview, podcast or AMA from Zack D. on scripting. All process details are secondhand.
- Exact Short lengths and narration speed for his Shorts were not measurable here, because the transcript page was paywalled (402) and no timed transcript was found.
- Per-Short view counts were not retrievable from trackers. The top-video lists appear to exclude Shorts.

## 2D AI/cartoon "glow-up" / transformation story series (Day 0 to Day 30 style) and serialized AI cartoon pages

### Takeaway
I could not find press or analytic coverage of a specific viral "Day 0 to Day 30" 2D cartoon weight-loss or glow-up page. Evidence for this sub-format is anecdotal and mostly comes from AI-tool vendors selling glow-up/progress templates. The best-documented analogue is the serialized AI microdrama *Fruit Love Island* (Mar 2026). It shows how episodic, cliffhanger-driven, recurring-character AI cartoon series can explode on TikTok: about 3M followers and about 300M views in roughly two weeks.

### Cited Findings
- **Fruit Love Island launch and creator:** An AI-generated microdrama parodying ITV's *Love Island* with fruit characters, run by the anonymous account @ai.cinema021 ("AI Cinema"). It launched on TikTok on March 13, 2026, and production stopped on March 28, 2026 after alleged mass-reporting. A finale was posted, and copycat accounts followed. — [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/ai-generated-dating-show-pulls-10-million-views-per-episode-on-tiktok/); [C21 Media](https://www.c21media.net/news/fruit-love-island-takes-social-media-by-storm-as-ai-microdramas-set-to-surge/); [The Triangle](https://thetriangle.org/article/the-rise-and-fall-of-fruit-love-island)
- **Fruit Love Island reach:** About 300M views and about 3M followers. Episodes frequently passed 10M views, and one report cites 3.1M followers and 27.9M likes across 21 episodes. The top episode, "New Dates... New Doubts," is reported at about 39M views. — [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/ai-generated-dating-show-pulls-10-million-views-per-episode-on-tiktok/); [SAYS](https://says.com/my/tech/ai-fruit-dating-tiktok); [Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2598994/ai-generated-fruit-love-island-videos-go-viral-on-tiktok)
- **Fruit Love Island format:** Vertical, mobile-first episodes of 2 to 4 minutes. It borrows a familiar show structure (coupling ceremonies, recouplings, cliffhanger eliminations). A host reads viewer comments to contestants, and viewers are invited to weigh in on pairings, so the audience participates in the plot. — [Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2598994/ai-generated-fruit-love-island-videos-go-viral-on-tiktok); [Blavity](https://blavity.com/fruit-love-island-series-tiktok)
- **Fruit Love Island criticism and risk:** Critics called it "AI slop," it raised IP questions about ITV's format, and the account was allegedly mass-reported. — [Comment BSU](https://commentbsu.com/2026/04/fruit-love-island-and-the-ai-slop-of-it-all/); [The Triangle](https://thetriangle.org/article/the-rise-and-fall-of-fruit-love-island)
- **AI story clip scale on Instagram:** MIT Technology Review cites the AI video "Auntlantis" at 13.5M views on Instagram as an example of an animated AI story clip at scale. — [MIT Technology Review, Dec 23 2025](https://www.technologyreview.com/2025/12/23/1130396/how-i-learned-to-stop-worrying-and-love-ai-slop/)
- **AI share of Shorts:** A New York Times analysis of Kapwing data, as relayed secondhand, reported that 20% of Shorts shown to first-time viewers were AI-generated, and that top AI channels earn over $4M per year. — [Verde Magazine](https://verdemagazine.com/cant-stop-the-slop-navigating-through-an-era-of-ai-generated-content)
- **Vendor templates (weak evidence of the trend):** Glow-up and progress templates are sold for Reels and TikTok, including "fitness progress layouts, beauty reveal formats, room makeover structures, and style evolution designs." Their existence suggests demand, but this is vendor-marketing evidence only. — [Hooked glow-up generator](https://www.hooked.so/tools/glow-up-transition-video-generator); [Revid glow-up tool](https://www.revid.ai/tools/glow-up-transition-video-generator); [CapCut Dreamina](https://dreamina.capcut.com/ai-video/ai-glow-up-video-generator)
- **AI animation idea guides:** These recommend animating popular transformation or POV challenges instead of filming them, plus "multi-part cliffhanger series" built to bring viewers back. Vendor blog. — [Anijam AI animation ideas (2026)](https://www.anijam.ai/blog/ai-animation-ideas/)
- **AI cartoon series workflow (vendor):** Fixed casts, set episode lengths, and up to six episodes per story arc. — [GenCartoon](https://gencartoon.com/blog/ai-cartoon-series)

### Inferences
These spread mechanics are inferred from Fruit Love Island and the general format logic, not from data on Day-N pages:
- **A visible progress counter** ("Day 0 / Day 7 / Day 30") is a built-in open loop. Each post promises a next state, so viewers follow or return to see the outcome. This is the same pull as cliffhanger eliminations.
- **A relatable, slightly flawed recurring character** lets viewers project themselves into the story.
- **Familiar structure** (a known show format, or the before/after arc) lowers the comprehension cost in the first second.
- **Audience participation** (comment-driven plot) feeds the comment and share signals.
- **Risks:** AI-slop backlash and mass-reporting, IP issues when borrowing formats or characters, and possible body-image criticism of weight-loss glow-ups. The last is not sourced; I found no reporting on it.
- **Fit for the owl page:** The "Day 0 to Day 30" counter maps naturally to the young-guy character in money or body topics, for example "Day 1 of saving $5/day" or "What 30 days without sugar does to your body." This would be a series wrapper around an explainer, with the owl as the explaining voice. It is a design suggestion, not an observed case.

### Gaps
- I found no named, verifiable Instagram or TikTok 2D cartoon "Day 0 to Day 30" glow-up page with documented follower or view numbers from 2025–2026. Searches returned only vendor tools and unrelated celebrity weight-loss news. Identifying specific pages would require searching hashtags inside TikTok or Instagram directly, which these tools cannot do.
- I found no data on posting cadence or part-to-part retention for serialized cartoon pages.

## Other relevant animated/faceless cases with large reach (2021–2026)

### Takeaway
The best-evidenced comparable cases are Jake Fellman (3D animated Shorts, about 9M subs by late 2021), Lessons in Meme Culture (fast-paced narrated explainers in under 3 minutes, about 2.2M subs), and the 3D medical animation channels (hundreds of millions of views, but far below Zack D.). I found no Shorts-specific evidence for Kurzgesagt, The Infographics Show, or animated finance pages.

### Cited Findings
- **Jake Fellman (3D animated Shorts):** As of December 14, 2021, he had nearly 9M subscribers, gaining about 25K per day, after starting Shorts in September 2020. His Shorts often took nearly a month to reach the first million views and kept gaining for about a year. Videos were usually under 60 seconds and often under 20. — [YouTube Official Blog, Dec 2021](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/11-tips-from-youtubes-fastest-growing-creator/)
- **Fellman's tips, in his words:** "There is no clickbaiting in short form." Titles, thumbnails and tags play "a very minor role." "Build a brand and niche down, but don't pigeonhole your channel." Try a new format every 6 to 8 months. "Pick something you can create in 15-30 minutes for your first 50 videos." "Quantity is more important than quality for beginners." — [YouTube Official Blog](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/11-tips-from-youtubes-fastest-growing-creator/)
- **Fellman loop result:** His seamless-loop Minecraft Short ("Minecraft RTX 24% Arrowhead Loop") reportedly did 15M views in 48 hours, against his usual best of 3 to 4M in 48 hours. — [vidIQ on looping Shorts (2021, reviewed 2026)](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/double-youtube-watch-time-looping-shorts/)
- **Fellman production effort:** A 15-second animation can take 2 to 3 full days (modeling, animating, rendering). — [The Futur masterclass summary](https://rosetta.to/u/thefutur/building-an-audience-of-20-million-in-1-year-masterclass-with-jake-fellman)
- **Fellman traffic and trends:** Only a small share of views came from subscribers or notifications, with most from the Shorts feed and suggested traffic. One analysis credits growth to pairing distinctive 3D animation with a trend (Among Us). — [vidIQ podcast summary](https://rosetta.to/u/vidiq/jake-fellman-and-the-secret-of-youtube-shorts)
- **Lessons in Meme Culture:** A "rapid-response encyclopedia" of viral internet phenomena in under 3 minutes, with "short-form-style pacing in a long-form format." Views are about 100% long-form per OutlierKit. Subscribers are about 2.23M (Social Blade) or 2.04M (vidIQ, April 2025 snapshot). It gained over 600K subs and 50M views in its first 6 months. — [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/channel/limc); [Know Your Meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/people/lessons-in-meme-culture); [Tubefilter, 2022](https://www.tubefilter.com/2022/09/08/youtube-millionaires-lessons-in-meme-culture/)
- **Kurzgesagt:** About 25.3M subscribers and about 3.7B total views, roughly 0.5 uploads per week, flat vector animation with "optimistic, existential framing." No Shorts-specific data was found. — [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/channel/kurzgesagt)
- **3D medical/body animation:** Dandelion Medical Animation has 530,292,144 total views and about 2.59M subs (2026-11-04). Medical Animation Media has 444,566,239 total views (2026-10-30), with conflicting history data. Both are far below Zack D. Films, which suggests the body niche wins at scale when packaged as curiosity ("what happens to your body if...") rather than clinical animation. That conclusion is inferred. — [vidIQ Dandelion](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/@dandelionmedicalanimation/); [vidIQ Medical Animation Media](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/@medicalanimationmedia/)
- **Animated finance:** Casual Finance (stick-figure finance explainers) reportedly grew to more than 300K subs in about a year, with view totals unverified (vendor-blog listing). Humphrey Yang (about 2.0M subs) uses Shorts to funnel into long-form but is not animated. The Plain Bagel uses animated charts plus stock footage plus narration. — [OutlierKit finance creators 2026](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-finance-niche-creators/)
- **Platform scale:** Shorts have earned over 9 trillion cumulative views as of Nov 21, 2025. — [vidIQ medical animation page (contextual figure)](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/@medicalanimationmedia/)

### Inferences
- **Clinical framing caps reach.** The gap between medical-animation channels (about 0.5B views) and Zack D. (about 82B) suggests body content scales when framed as a hypothetical or surprising question with second-person stakes, not as a clinical explainer. This is directional, since the channels differ in many ways.
- **Short, continuous loops.** Fellman's under-20-second loops and Zack D.'s calm continuous narration both point toward short, self-contained Shorts in which the end re-enters the start.

- I found no Shorts-specific stats or scripting statements for Kurzgesagt or The Infographics Show.
- I found no verifiable large-reach (100M+ views) 2D cartoon finance Shorts page in 2024–2026.
- I found no "MrBeast-style" animated page with verifiable numbers.

## What creators and strategists say about scripting Shorts (Jenny Hoyos, Paddy Galloway, Jake Fellman)

### Takeaway
The most concrete, first-party scripting system comes from Jenny Hoyos (Creator Science podcast, Oct 2023):
- Write the hook and the last line first.
- Foreshadow the payoff in two lines in 3 seconds or less.
- Drive the body with "but/therefore" beats.
- Write at a 5th-grade-or-lower reading level.
- End immediately after the payoff.
- Aim for about 34 seconds and 90%+ retention.

Paddy Galloway's 3.3B-view study (2023) adds that Shorts of 40 seconds or more that hold viewers tend to win, and that the first second must be treated "like a thumbnail."

### Cited Findings
- **Hoyos script order:** Pick the idea, write the hook and the last line, foreshadow, rough script or bullets, film, revise, edit, then voiceover. Body structure is problem-solution or "but, therefore" storytelling, ending "abrupt." — [Creator Science Ep. 167, Oct 10 2023](https://creatorscience.com/podcast/jenny-hoyos/)
- **Hoyos hook test:** A hook is good "if it could be used as a title and thumbnail on a long form and... it will still get clicks." It must be visual, understandable without audio, and simple, and she sketches the visual on an iPad before writing the line. — [Creator Science](https://creatorscience.com/podcast/jenny-hoyos/)
- **Hoyos reading level:** She targets 5th grade or lower, checked on readabilityformulas.com. She says scraped popular Shorts (including MrBeast's) score around 1st grade, and that words like "business" or "profit" push scripts toward 8th grade, so she explains terms instead. — [Creator Science](https://creatorscience.com/podcast/jenny-hoyos/)
- **Hoyos foreshadowing:** Two lines in 3 seconds or less, every video, for example "Chick-fil-A has the best chicken sandwiches, but I am not paying $6." After the foreshadow she avoids adding extra details (citing primacy/recency). — [Creator Science](https://creatorscience.com/podcast/jenny-hoyos/); [Marketing Examined, May 2024](https://www.marketingexamined.com/blog/jenny-hoyos-short-form-video-playbook)
- **Hoyos length and endings:** Her average Short is about 34 seconds, the length of her most popular videos. "Whatever you say you're gonna do, you end it right after you do it." Cutting 1 second from a 32-second Short changes retention math by about 3%. In one example, trimming a final second that dropped from 70% to 45% raised overall retention from 83% to 88%, and the video grew. — [Creator Science](https://creatorscience.com/podcast/jenny-hoyos/)
- **Hoyos retention benchmarks:** About 90%+ retention is the "bare minimum" to take off, and her average is about 95%, helped by rewatches. Average scroll-through (viewed vs. swiped) is about 70%, against her about 85%. — [Creator Science](https://creatorscience.com/podcast/jenny-hoyos/)
- **Hoyos endings and pacing (secondhand):** End on a high: the funniest punchline, the most heartwarming moment, or the biggest twist, leaving viewers "satisfied, but surprised." — [Marketing Examined](https://www.marketingexamined.com/blog/jenny-hoyos-short-form-video-playbook). Pacing alternates fast hook, medium middle, fast peak, medium resolution and fast ending (Peak-End theory). She also uses "content buckets," repeatable formats with similar titles such as "$1 fast food item vs restaurant." — [vidIQ, Nov 2023](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/how-jenny-hoyos-gets-10m-views-per-youtube-short/)
- **Hoyos power words:** "banned," "free," "one dollar," "secret," "cheap." Reach was about 3M subs and about 10M average views per Short as of May 2024. — [Marketing Examined](https://www.marketingexamined.com/blog/jenny-hoyos-short-form-video-playbook)
- **Paddy Galloway (3.3B-view Shorts study, 2023):** The algorithm "appear[s] to favour longer videos" of about 40 seconds or more that hold viewers well, though outliers exist at all lengths. "Viewed vs. swiped away" is key, so make the first second punchy and "Treat your intro like a thumbnail." Likes, comments and shares didn't appear "massively important" in initial results. Shorts averaged 16.9 subs per 10K views and about $0.06 RPM. — [Galloway LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/paddy-galloway-459b8913a_we-studied-33-billion-views-to-decode-the-activity-7053697597592498176-PAkf); [Galloway on X](https://x.com/PaddyG96/status/1646898356419981315)
- **Galloway, broader:** The intro's job is to make "the viewer feel they made the right choice by clicking." Study "outlier" videos (3x a channel's average) for an hour a day. — [search summary of Galloway posts](https://x.com/PaddyG96/status/1803096396083396633)
- **Fellman:** Content beats optimization, so no clickbait and minimal title/thumbnail effort. Start simple (15–30-minute videos for the first 50), refresh the format every 6–8 months, and use seamless visual and audio loops by matching the first and last frames. — [YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/11-tips-from-youtubes-fastest-growing-creator/); [vidIQ looping](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/double-youtube-watch-time-looping-shorts/)
- **YouTube view counting:** Shorts views now count replays with no minimum watch time, while "engaged views" exclude loops. — [vidIQ looping](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/double-youtube-watch-time-looping-shorts/)

### Inferences
- **Hoyos's system matches Zack D.'s scripts.** Her stunt-based system and his explainer scripts converge on the same skeleton: a visual question or premise hook, an implied payoff, but/so beats, and an end right at the payoff.
- **The reading-level rule matters for money topics.** Jargon ("compound interest," "inflation") must be explained in kid words or shown visually.
- **Galloway's "about 40s+" and Hoyos's "about 34s" ranges are compatible.** Both point to a 30–45 second sweet spot, with length earned only by retention. The figures date from 2023, and the algorithm may have changed since.

### Gaps
- I found no first-party Zack D. interview.
- Galloway's 2024–2026 Shorts commentary was not retrieved, so his cited data is from 2023.
- Hoyos's statement that "under 30s needs over 100% retention" may be a transcript error.

## Typical lengths and words-per-second of narration

### Takeaway
Evidence points to 30–45 second Shorts for story and explainer formats: Hoyos averages about 34 seconds, and Galloway found about 40s+ favored. Pure-visual loop animations can run under 20 seconds (Fellman). Narration planning ranges cluster at 140–160 wpm, about 2.3–2.7 words per second, so a 34-second script is about 80–90 words. These pace figures come from vendor and tool sources, not measured viral Shorts.

### Cited Findings
- **Story and explainer Shorts:** Hoyos's average Short is about 34 seconds. — [Creator Science](https://creatorscience.com/podcast/jenny-hoyos/)
- **Study range:** Shorts of about 40 seconds or more that hold viewers performed better in Galloway's 2023 3.3B-view study. — [Galloway LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/paddy-galloway-459b8913a_we-studied-33-billion-views-to-decode-the-activity-7053697597592498176-PAkf)
- **Animation loops:** Fellman's animated Shorts were usually under 60 seconds and often under 20. — [YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/11-tips-from-youtubes-fastest-growing-creator/)
- **Narrated explainer comparison:** Lessons in Meme Culture explainers run under 3 minutes (long-form). — [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/channel/limc)
- **Serialized AI microdrama comparison:** Fruit Love Island episodes ran 2–4 minutes on TikTok. — [Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2598994/ai-generated-fruit-love-island-videos-go-viral-on-tiktok)
- **Speaking-rate guidance (vendor and tool sources, weak evidence):**
  - 160–180 wpm for Shorts narration. — [Voxbooster](https://voxbooster.com/blog/ai-voice-generator-youtube-shorts-narration)
  - About 150 wpm as natural narration. — [CollabPals script timer](https://www.collabpals.com/tools/youtube-script-timer)
  - About 2.5 words per second (about 150 wpm). — [FlowShorts](https://flowshorts.app/blog/words-per-minute-speaking)
  - Above 160 wpm becomes hard to follow, and below 120 feels sluggish. — [LetterCounter](https://lettercounter.org/blog/video-script-word-count/)
  - 120–140 wpm for instructional content. — [Teleprompter.com](https://teleprompter.com/blog/how-to-time-your-script)

### Inferences
- **Word budgets:**
  - 30s ≈ 70–80 words
  - 34s ≈ 80–90 words
  - 45s ≈ 105–120 words

  These are at about 2.4–2.6 words per second. A calm Zack-D-style narrator likely sits near 150 wpm with pauses at the "But" and "So" turns; that is anecdotal and not measured.
- **Owl versus young guy pacing:** Explainer content with a mascot narrator (the owl) can sit at about 2.3–2.5 words per second. The young guy's reaction lines can be faster and shorter.

### Gaps
- No measured words-per-second data exists for Zack D. Films or other top animated Shorts. A practical fix is to time 10–20 of his Shorts manually (word count divided by duration).
- No measured length distribution exists for Zack D. Shorts.

## Synthesis: patterns most transferable to a 2D cartoon explainer page (owl narrator + young guy; money, how things work, the body; US audience)

### Takeaway
Combine Zack D.'s question, rule, "But" twist and "So" consequence skeleton with Hoyos's production rules:
- Write the hook and last line first.
- Make the hook understandable on mute.
- Foreshadow in 3 seconds or less.
- Keep to a 5th-grade reading level.
- End right after the payoff.

Wrap this in a recurring-character series device, such as a "Day N" counter or a recurring question format, at about 30–45 seconds and about 75–110 words.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
Every item below is a recommendation inferred from the cases above, not tested data.

1. **Hook formulas.** Each should be speakable in under 2 seconds and visible on mute:
   - "What happens to your body if you [X]?"
   - "If you [put $1 / skipped sleep / swallowed gum]..."
   - "How does [ATM / credit score / microwave] actually work?"
   - "Why [prices / your stomach] [does X]?"
   - Power words: free, secret, $1, banned, cheap.
   - On screen, the young guy is already doing the risky or relatable thing in frame 1.
2. **Beat structure (about 80–100 words):**
   - Hook question (young guy's situation)
   - 1-line foreshadow ("...and the answer is not what you think")
   - "Well," the common-sense rule (owl)
   - "But" the twist (visual reveal)
   - "So" the consequence with a concrete number or US-relevant detail
   - Optional second "But" escalation
   - Payoff line, then cut immediately
3. **Narration roles.** The owl is a calm, second-person explainer ("you'd...") at about 2.4 words per second. The young guy gets short reaction lines and acts out the hypothetical, which adds character comedy without slowing the explanation. Keep owl lines at a 5th-grade level and translate finance jargon into objects (for example, a snowball for compound interest).
4. **Visuals-to-words.** One literal visual per sentence. Use close-ups and cutaways inside the body (Zack D.'s extreme close-up style, translated into 2D cross-sections), and use numbers on screen for money topics. Every line should still make sense with sound off.
5. **Endings and loops.** End on the payoff, with no outro or "follow for more" tail (Hoyos's trimmed final second added 5 points of retention). Optionally write the last line to flow back into the hook (for example, "...which is why you should never [hook action]") and match the first and last frames for a seamless replay. An optional ending question to the viewer drives comments.
6. **Series formats:**
   - Repeatable title buckets such as "What happens to your body if...", "How does ___ actually work?" and "$1 vs $100 ___".
   - A "Day N" counter arc for the young guy (savings challenge, habit change), so each Short contains a self-contained explainer plus a progress hook to the next episode.
   - Refresh the format every 6–8 months (Fellman).
7. **Length and cadence.** Aim for 30–45 seconds and add length only where retention holds. Post daily or more often to test hooks, since the evidence favors high volume. Track viewed-vs-swiped above about 70% (Hoyos's average benchmark) and retention near or above 90%.
8. **Avoid:**
   - Copying any channel's characters or IP; borrowed formats triggered the Fruit Love Island IP questions and reporting.
   - Slop signals (uncanny AI faces, mismatched audio).
   - Body-shaming in glow-up arcs, which is a reputational risk. That last point is inferred and unsourced.

### Gaps
- No A/B data was found that compares owl-narrator mascot formats with faceless voice-only formats.
- There is no US-specific evidence on which of money, how-things-work, or body topics performs best in animated Shorts; this needs channel-level testing.
