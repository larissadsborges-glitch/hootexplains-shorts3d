## Verified facts
- At cruise the cabin is pressurised to about 10–11 psi (a cabin altitude of about 8,000 ft). Outside it's about 3.5 psi, so the difference is about 7.5–8 psi.
- Airliner doors are **plug doors**: bigger than the frame, wedge-shaped, and they must first move **inward** before swinging out. Cabin pressure pushes them shut, and the higher you fly, the tighter the seal.
  Source: [AirlineRatings](https://www.airlineratings.com/articles/can-you-open-an-aircraft-door-mid-flight).
- Force on an A320-size door is about 0.81 m × 1.85 m ≈ 1.5 m² ≈ 2,325 in² × 7.5 psi ≈ **17,000+ lb (~7,900 kg)**, about the weight of 4 cars. No human can pull that.
- **Twist (true story):** on 26 May 2023, on Asiana Airbus A321 approaching Daegu at **~700 ft**, a passenger opened the emergency exit. Near the ground there's almost no pressure difference, so the door opened. Wind roared through the cabin. 194 people were on board, 12 had minor injuries, and the plane landed safely. The passenger was arrested.
  Source: [PBS NewsHour / AP](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/amp/world/passenger-opens-exit-door-during-flight-in-south-korea).
