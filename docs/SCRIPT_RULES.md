# Hoot Explains: scriptwriting rulebook

Every idea, title and script is checked against this before any production starts. The sources are this conversation's research plus our own video data (see the learnings file, the game plan and the competitor scan).

## A. Choosing the idea (before writing a word)
1. **Stakes or a "no way" fact.** Danger, money, your own body, huge scale, or a thing everyone has seen but never understood ("why does the eye twitch?"). A plain "how X works" with nothing at stake is weak.
2. **A "you" connection.** The viewer must think "this is about me": your phone, your sleep, your money, your flight.
3. **Built to be shared** (Jonah Berger's STEPPS research). People share what:
   - makes them look smart (social currency)
   - is useful to someone they know (practical value)
   - triggers a high-arousal emotion: awe, surprise, fear or outrage. Sad and calm content spreads less (Berger & Milkman, 2012, a study of New York Times most-emailed articles).
   - comes back to mind in daily life (triggers: every flight, every all-nighter)
   - is a story

   **Test:** name the exact person a viewer would send it to ("the friend who never turns on airplane mode"). If you can't, pick another idea.
4. **Proven before it's ours:** prefer topics that have already worked on other pages (competitor scan), then tell them better with our angle (Jake + Hoot, a story, a twist).
5. **Series-able?** If it can become Day 1/2/3 or Part 1/2, that's a follow reason.

## B. Title and cover
6. **Statement with a twist or a sharp question,** in plain words. No clickbait the answer contradicts. "Pilots can hear your phone" beats "Why airplane mode?". "The plane has 180 seats. They sold 190." beats "What is overbooking?"
7. **The cover is the first frame:** a 2–3 line title with one accent-coloured word, Hoot plus the subject, and our fixed template.

## C. The first 3 seconds (the hook)
8. **Start mid-action or with the payoff on screen.** Never a greeting, logo or "today we'll talk about". The opening image shows the most surprising moment (Jake asleep on the desk at hour 24, the plane window cracking).
9. **One hook line, under ~12 words,** with the title phrase (for search), a stake and a gap. Formulas that work:
   - **Outcome first:** "In 24 hours, his brain works like a drunk driver's."
   - **Contrarian:** "Your phone won't crash the plane. That's not why they ban it."
   - **Specific number:** "17 hours awake. That's 0.05% blood alcohol."
   - **"You" question with stakes:** "What happens if you don't sleep for 3 days?"
   - **Weird observation:** "Why does your eye twitch for no reason?"
10. **On-screen text matches the spoken hook,** and captions are visible from the first second (most people watch muted).
11. **Don't say what the picture already shows.** Narration adds; it doesn't describe.

## D. The body (holding attention)
12. **"But / therefore", never "and then"** (Trey Parker & Matt Stone). Each beat is caused by the last ("therefore") or complicates it ("but"). A list of facts is not a story.
13. **Open loops:** ask a question and delay the answer ("but there's one moment when it CAN open…"), and close it later. Have one loop open at all times.
14. **A re-hook every 10–15 s:** "But that's not the scary part." / "Then something weird happens." Restate the stakes before the big reveal.
15. **A new visual every ~2 s** (a cut, camera move, label, counter or expression), and a new beat every 4–7 s. Write the visual next to every line.
16. **Every line must do one of four jobs:** create curiosity, raise stakes, deliver value, or set up the next beat. Cut everything else.
17. **Be specific:** numbers, names, times, places ("3 a.m.", "0.10%", "Asiana flight, 700 feet"). Turn each number into a counter or comparison people can feel ("longer than a football field", "like 4 cars on the door").
18. **Myth, then truth:** state what people believe, then flip it.
19. **Use a character:** Jake lives the story and Hoot explains it. Put "you" in it ("imagine 200 phones doing that during landing").
20. **Speakable sentences:** about 125–150 words per minute (≈2.3 words per second at our speed). Short sentences, no jargon in the first 5 s, no dotted abbreviations. Read it aloud; rewrite any line that makes you stumble.

## E. Ending and CTA
21. **The main follow or series CTA goes mid-video at peak action** ("follow, because day 2 is worse"). People leave right after the payoff (Jenny Hoyos).
22. **End on the punchline and cut.** No lingering outro. The final line or image ideally loops back to the first frame.
23. **At most one closing ask, and make it specific:** a decision question that turns comments into votes ("Could you make it to day 2? Yes or no?"), or "send this to the friend who…". Avoid generic "follow for more" and comment bait as the main tactic.
24. **Payoff before any ask.** Resolve the hook's question first.

## F. Length and pacing
25. **Default 30–45 s; story topics up to 60 s.** Test a 30–35 s cut as a trial reel. Shorter is easier to keep above 80% watched.
26. Over 60 s only if every 10 s brings a new twist.

## G. Facts and trust
27. **Every claim is sourced** (official, medical or primary sources), with a source chip on screen for health, money and safety, and sources listed in the post kit.
28. **Health:** "don't try this". **Money:** "not financial advice", teach principles, no specific stocks or crypto, no luxury flexing.

## H. Pre-production checklist (run on every script)
- [ ] Who exactly would this be sent to? (names a person)
- [ ] Does the first frame show the payoff or action? Is the hook line under 12 words with the title phrase?
- [ ] Is every beat linked by "but" or "therefore"? Is a loop open at every moment?
- [ ] Is there a re-hook every 10–15 s and a visual change every ~2 s (written per line)?
- [ ] Is every number made concrete (counter, comparison)?
- [ ] Is the CTA mid-video, the ending on the punchline, and the loop back to frame one?
- [ ] Are all facts sourced? Is the safety or money line included?
- [ ] Read aloud: no stumbles, about 2.3 words per second, length on target?
- [ ] Does the title/cover follow the template, with a twist statement or a sharp "you" question?

## I. 2026 research update (5 Nov 2026)

**Hook and bridge**
- Name the familiar subject (coffee, paycheck, microwave, credit score) in the **first 3–5 spoken words AND on screen** before any twist. Shock without a named subject is banned.
- On-screen hook text is **3–5 bold words that match the spoken hook** exactly. First frame, text and VO all point at the same single question.
- Frame 1 shows the payoff's **subject and stakes**, never the answer itself.
- A **bridge line by ~3–6 s** states the conflict and what the viewer will know by the end. No history, backstory or date may appear before the bridge.
- **Timeline topics:** never open on a date. Open on the present-day consequence, bridge, then 2–4 dated beats, each tied back to "you".
- Jake voices the common belief or mistake (lean-in). The owl delivers the "But…" snapback.
- Phrase the first sentence as a **US search query** where possible ("Why does…", "How does… actually work"), spoken and shown on screen.

**Structure and timing**
- Write the **hook and the last line first**, then the middle.
- Default skeleton: **Question → "Well" (intuitive rule) → "But" (twist) → "So" (consequence with a number)**.
- A small **first payoff by ~6–12 s**. The main answer lands in the **last 15–25%** as one plain on-screen sentence.
- One "but/therefore" turn every **4–8 s**. At most **one** false payoff per video.
- **Cut within ~1 s of the payoff/punchline.** No recap, no "follow for more", no end card.
- Closing question goes in **on-screen text over the final frame plus the caption or pinned comment**, or folds into Jake's last line as the loop. It is never a separate spoken tail.
- **Modular build:** a 35–45 s core for Reels and Shorts, plus a 15–25 s "bonus But" inserted *before* the payoff for a TikTok 61 s+ cut.

**Words, voice, captions**
- **5th-grade reading level or lower** (run a readability check). Gloss every finance or body term in kid words *and* with a visual on the same word.
- **Word budget ≈ 2.5 words/s:** 30 s ≈ 75, 40 s ≈ 100, 45 s ≈ 110, 61 s ≈ 150. Slow the one "aha" line. Never sustain above ~180 wpm.
- Fill 0.3–0.6 s beats before reveals and numbers with a visual or SFX, not silence.
- Captions show **2–4 words grouped by phrase** with the spoken word highlighted, plus one accent color only for key numbers.
- Keep captions and faces inside a **centered ~900×1400 px safe box**, with the right ~140 px clear. Check each app's draft preview.
- Every key number appears as a **big isolated graphic**. No audio-only puns. The video must read muted like a silent comic.

**Distribution hygiene**
- Script **one sendable line at or before the midpoint**: a specific "wait, what?" fact, or a "that's so [friend]" moment.
- No engagement bait ("comment YES", "tag 3 friends", "like if"). CTAs are natural send prompts.
- Clean, watermark-free native export per platform. Original or cleared audio only (mandatory for >60 s Shorts). At most 3–5 hashtags.
- Never AI-clone or depict a real, identifiable person.
- A/B test hooks with **Trial Reels**. Judge by IG View Rate, YT viewed-vs-swiped and engaged views, and TikTok finish rate, against the channel's own rolling median and over weeks, not 48 h. Directional bar: <60% viewed-vs-swiped means fix the hook first.

These rules come from `reports/Viral short video scripting.md`; its notes are in `research_notes/Viral short video scripting/`. Where they conflict with the older rules above, these win:
- Rule 23: the closing question is shown on screen over the final frame, or folded into the last line, never a separate spoken tail.
- Rule 15: the 2-second visual cadence is a habit, not a proven number.
- **Exception for vote-driven series (A Vida do Tavinho):** the vote IS the story. Phrase it as a real story choice ("Você decide: VÍDEO ou VÓ?") with the voting rules in the pinned comment. Never use generic bait ("comenta SIM", "marca 3 amigos").
