# Sound-off/Sound-on Writing, Captions, Voiceover, Audio, and Scripting for Sends and Comments (for a 2D cartoon explainer page, owl AI narrator plus young guy, US, 30-60 s, word-by-word captions)

Research date: 2026-11-05. Confidence flags: [HIGH] = platform primary doc or academic study; [MED] = credible trade press or vendor-commissioned research with stated method; [LOW] = vendor/SEO blog, unpublished method, or recycled stat.

## 1. What share of short-form viewers watch muted, and how should text and visuals carry the story when muted?

### Takeaway
I found no credible 2024-2026 number for the share of Reels/TikTok/Shorts views watched muted. The famous "85%" and "92%" figures are 2016-2019 and either self-reported or not specific to short-form. TikTok's own (vendor) research says its audience is sound-on. Design for both: assume sound-on is the main experience on TikTok/Shorts, but make every beat readable muted, because a meaningful minority watches in public or with sound off.

### Cited Findings
- The widely repeated "85% of videos are watched without sound" traces to a 2016 trade article in which two publishers self-reported it. No platform has confirmed it. [LOW/OUTDATED, do not use] — [Vidico, short-form video statistics](https://vidico.com/news/short-form-video-statistics/)
- Verizon Media and Publicis Media, "Relationship of Video, Sound and Captions Insights Study" (April 2019), a survey of US consumers (one report says more than 5,600). Findings: 69% watch video with sound off in public places, about 25% in private places, and about 80% say they are more likely to watch a whole video when captions are available. The sample size appears only in secondary coverage. [MED, OUTDATED 2019, self-reported, predates the Reels/Shorts era] — [3Play Media summary](https://www.3playmedia.com/blog/verizon-media-and-publicis-media-find-viewers-want-captions/); [Next TV](https://www.nexttv.com/news/mobile-videos-often-watched-without-audio-study-finds); [StreamingMedia, "80% of caption users aren't hearing impaired"](https://www.streamingmedia.com/Articles/News/Online-Video-News/80-of-Video-Caption-Users-Arent-Hearing-Impaired-Finds-Verizon-131860.aspx)
- The "92% watch mobile video with sound off" figure, attributed to the same 2019 research family, is recycled in 2026 roundups without a platform breakdown. [LOW] — [Vidico](https://vidico.com/news/short-form-video-statistics/); [Digiday (older "75% on mute")](https://digiday.com/sponsored/75-percent-of-people-watch-mobile-videos-on-mute/?app=1)
- TikTok/Kantar, "US Marketing Science Cross-Platform Sound Research" (January 2021): 88% of TikTok users said sound is essential to the TikTok experience, and 73% said they would "stop and look" at TikTok ads with audio. This is TikTok-commissioned, self-reported, and not replicated. [MED, vendor, 2021] — [TikTok for Business, Kantar report](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/kantar-report-how-brands-are-making-noise-and-driving-impact-with-sound-on-tiktok); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/tiktok-shares-new-insights-into-the-importance-of-sound-for-marketing-promo/601569)
- TVB, 2025: 38% of respondents watch TV muted, on earbuds or hearing aids, or with closed captions. This is a TV statistic, not short-form, but it is evidence that captions-on viewing is now normal. [MED, adjacent context] — [NewscastStudio, Nov 2025](https://www.newscaststudio.com/2025/11/13/nearly-40-percent-of-viewers-watch-tv-with-sound-off-raising-measurement-concerns/); [TV News Check](https://tvnewscheck.com/tech/article/new-study-finds-38-of-respondents-view-tv-with-speaker-sound-off/)
- A YouGov study of short-form video and TV viewing (fielded Oct 20-27, 2025; 2,035 GB adults; published March 2026) exists, but I could not extract any sound-off findings, and it covers the UK, not the US. [unverified] — referenced in [search roundup, Vidico](https://vidico.com/news/short-form-video-statistics/)

### Inferences
- The defensible framing for the report: "There is no reliable current platform-level muted-viewing share. Older surveys (2019) suggest most people mute in public, while TikTok's own research says its users treat sound as essential." Do not quote 85% or 92% as current fact.
- Muted-viewing rules for this page (practitioner logic, not tested):
  - (a) The hook must work as text plus visual alone. Put a 3-7 word headline-style question or claim on screen in the first second, for example "Why your paycheck shrinks", plus a visual "problem" image such as the guy staring at his pay stub.
  - (b) Every key number or term in the script should appear on screen as a big, isolated visual, such as "$1,000 becomes $674" or an organ label, not only in the caption line.
  - (c) Let the characters' acting carry emotion (the guy's shocked or confused face, the owl pointing) so the cartoon reads like a silent comic.
  - (d) Word-by-word captions do double duty as subtitles for muted viewers.
- Because TikTok and Shorts lean sound-on, the voice, SFX, and music still matter for retention. Do not write "silent-first" videos that undercut the narrator.

### Gaps
- No platform (Meta, TikTok, YouTube) has published 2024-2026 sound-on/sound-off share for short-form feeds that I could find.
- I could not verify the Verizon/Publicis sample size or methodology from the primary source (the original Verizon Media insights page and Forbes write-up were not accessible).

## 2. Caption best practices: word-by-word vs phrase, size, placement, safe zones, highlight words, readability, and how much text is too much

### Takeaway
No peer-reviewed study compares word-by-word animated captions with phrase captions for short-form retention. The claimed 10-50% lifts are vendor numbers with unpublished methods. Broadcast subtitle standards (BBC 160-180 wpm; Netflix about 17-20 characters per second for English adults, 42 characters per line, 2 lines maximum) give hard readability ceilings. Safe zones are not officially published for organic posts. The conservative cross-platform default is to keep captions and key art in a centered area of about 900x1400 px on a 1080x1920 frame, with the right edge clear.

### Cited Findings
- Animated or word-by-word caption performance claims: "10-20% higher watch time" (unnamed creator community tests), "18-23%" (no source), and a vendor test of 7 styles across 80 videos showing a 30-50% swing (method unpublished). [LOW] — [BlitzCut, animated vs static captions](https://blitzcutai.com/blog/animated-captions-vs-static-captions); [dev.to / Clamper](https://dev.to/clamper_ai/how-ai-caption-styles-affect-watch-time-and-engagement-409i); [Alcheclip word-level vs line-level](https://alcheclip.vercel.app/guides/word-level-vs-line-level-captions)
- Academic subtitle eye-tracking work finds that viewers prefer syntactically segmented line breaks (breaking lines at phrase boundaries). The same literature notes that standard eye-tracking metrics struggle with dynamic subtitle text. None of it tests word-by-word animation. [HIGH for line-break finding, adjacent] — [core.ac.uk eye-tracking subtitles paper](https://core.ac.uk/download/158974629.pdf); [PlanetRead, subtitles, reading, eye tracking](https://betatest.planetread.org/pdf/Subtitles%2C%20reading%2C%20eye%20tracking.pdf)
- Reading-speed standards: BBC recommends 160-180 words per minute and 37 characters per line for broadcast. Netflix English is most often cited at 20 characters per second for adults and 17 for children's content, with 42 characters per line, 2 lines maximum, and a minimum event of 5/6 of a second. Secondary sources conflict, and one cites 17 cps for adults. Netflix confirms each language has its own guide but I did not verify the English numbers on the primary page. [MED] — [Netflix Timed Text Style Guide hub](https://backlothelp.netflix.com/hc/en-us/articles/215758617); [Subtitle reading speed guide](https://subtitle-toolset.pages.dev/guides/subtitle-reading-speed/); [Languages Unlimited summary of the Netflix guide](https://www.languagesunlimited.com/?p=17279)
- Safe zones:
  - Meta, Google, and TikTok publish safe-zone resources mainly for ads. They are not permanent rules for organic posts. [MED] — [Reap, safe zones](https://reap.video/blog/short-form-video-safe-zones)
  - Vendor margin tables conflict:
    - Revid: TikTok about 220 px top, 480 px bottom, 60 px left, 140 px right (an 880x1220 safe area). Reels about 270 px top and 670 px bottom.
    - Another vendor: TikTok bottom 250 px, right 100 px, top 100 px.
    - [LOW] — [Revid TikTok safe zone](https://www.revid.ai/tiktok-safe-zone); [AdaptlyPost safe zones 2026](https://adaptlypost.com/en/blog/social-media-safe-zones-2026-complete-guide)
  - A commonly recommended universal box is 900x1400 px centered in a 1080x1920 frame. An alternative rule keeps critical content between Y=220 and Y=1440. Reels and Shorts have larger bottom overlays than TikTok, and the right-side buttons are the usual collision point. [LOW-MED, consensus across vendors] — [Reap](https://reap.video/blog/short-form-video-safe-zones); [Wild and Free Tools, vertical safe zones 2026](https://wildandfreetools.com/blog/vertical-video-safe-zones-guide-2026/); [BlitzCut caption placement](https://blitzcutai.com/blog/best-caption-placement-short-form-video)
  - Preview in each app before publishing. TikTok drafts show the full UI overlay. [MED] — [Reap](https://reap.video/blog/short-form-video-safe-zones)

### Inferences
Rules for this page (based on the standards above plus practitioner consensus; A/B test them):
- **Caption style.** Keep word-by-word captions, but show 2-4 words at a time, grouped by phrase ("your paycheck" / "gets taxed" / "three times"), with the active word highlighted. Flashing one word at a time breaks phrase-level reading, and the eye-tracking literature favors phrase-segmented lines. Use one highlight color for the spoken word and a second accent color only for key terms or numbers, at most about one per sentence.
- **Size and font.** Bold sans-serif with a dark stroke or shadow, about 60-80 px cap height on a 1080 px-wide canvas. Keep lines to roughly 20-25 characters or fewer (well under the 37-42 broadcast limits, because phones are small and the background is busy animation). [inference]
- **Placement.** Captions sit in the vertical middle-to-lower-middle band, around Y about 1000-1350 px. Keep them out of the bottom about 480-670 px (the caption/description UI area; Reels is worst) and the right about 140 px (buttons). Put the owl's and guy's faces above the caption band. Use one placement that is safe on all three platforms, because the same file will be cross-posted.
- **How much text is too much.** Keep combined reading load (caption plus on-screen labels) under about 160-180 wpm of reading. At most one label or callout on screen besides the caption at any moment. Never have a full-sentence title and a caption competing; if a key number is shown big, the caption can drop that word or dim.

### Gaps
- No controlled or peer-reviewed study of word-by-word vs phrase captions in short-form.
- No official organic-content safe-zone pixel specs from Instagram, TikTok, or YouTube. I did not fetch the platforms' ad safe-zone templates directly.

## 3. Voiceover pacing, pauses, energy, AI vs human voice perception, and AI-disclosure rules and reach

### Takeaway
No platform-specific study links words per minute to short-form retention. Comprehension research shows little loss until speech approaches about 2x normal (about 275 wpm). Creator guides cluster at 150-165 wpm for Shorts/TikTok (up to about 170-200 for high-energy content), and broadcast caption readability tops out at about 180 wpm. For a cartoon explainer, YouTube, TikTok, and Meta all treat non-realistic or animated content as not requiring a prominent AI label. YouTube and TikTok state that disclosure does not reduce reach. Listeners often cannot tell AI from human voices, but stated trust drops when they know a voice is AI.

### Cited Findings
**Pacing**
- UCLA study: video speed had little effect on immediate or delayed comprehension until learners went beyond 2x speed. Normal speech is about 150 wpm, and comprehension starts to decline near 275 wpm. This is lecture context. [HIGH, adjacent] — [UCLA Newsroom](https://newsroom.ucla.edu/releases/learning-while-speed-watching-class-videos)
- Creator guidance: 150-165 wpm for Shorts/TikTok-style content and 135-150 wpm for standard YouTube explainers. Another guide says most creators land at 140-160 wpm, with 120-140 for tutorials and 170-200 for energetic short-form. [LOW] — [Ricolandia, video speaking rate](https://www.ricolandia.com/en/blog/video-speaking-rate/); [GoTeleprompter WPM guide](https://goteleprompter.com/blog/words-per-minute-speaking-rate-guide/); [VirtualSpeech, average speaking rate](https://virtualspeech.com/blog/average-speaking-rate-words-per-minute)
- An arXiv preprint notes that playback-speed studies have focused on lectures, not TikTok-style content mixing speech and music, so evidence for short-form specifically is thin. [HIGH, gap statement] — [arXiv CAPQ-FAST](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.03498)

**AI vs human voice perception**
- Studio Resonate (SiriusXM) test with fictitious brands: human voices drove 24% more brand attraction and scored 23% higher on trust than AI voices. Trust dropped when listeners were primed to expect AI, even for human voices. [MED, vendor] — [ContentGrip on Studio Resonate](https://www.contentgrip.com/studio-resonate-ai-voice-study/)
- Crowd React Media (1,326 US radio listeners): no statistically significant ability to tell human from AI voices before the reveal. After disclosure, 20% felt less favorable and 25% more favorable. [MED] — [Inside Radio](https://www.insideradio.com/free/study-finds-ai-voices-equal-humans-on-sound-not-trust/article_b8187d55-51da-49a3-8b40-ad1278cac6ad.html)
- WPP Media/Choreograph neuroscience work: respondents said they preferred human voices, but ads with synthetic voiceovers matched human-voiced ads on brand engagement and purchase intent. [MED] — [WPP Media, AI voices in audio ads](https://www.wppmedia.com/news/ai-voices-audio-ads)
- Adobe Express survey: 77% trust human voices most, and 14% distrust any voice they believe is AI. Acceptance is lowest for news (19%) and podcasts (9%). [MED, vendor survey] — [ContentGrip on Adobe](https://www.contentgrip.com/ai-voices-in-marketing-adobe/)
- Audiobook experiments (2025, including N=500, German market): effects were modest, and AI voices were described as a "viable substitute" from the listener's view. [MED, academic abstracts] — [Uni Münster conference abstract](https://indico.uni-muenster.de/event/3286/contributions/5732/); [Univ. Vienna publication](https://viecer.univie.ac.at/en/publications/detailseite-publikationen/pure/1926ebaf-68a6-47b4-949c-01b7eba83587/show/publ/Pure/)
- *Computers in Human Behavior Reports* (Aug 2025): synthetic voices could be rated more trustworthy than neutrally spoken human voices, but human voices with deliberately trustworthy delivery scored highest. Seen via secondary listing only. [MED] — [DOAJ listing](https://doaj.org/article/347460ae52be4188b9a35f1085f74052)

**Platform AI disclosure rules**
- **YouTube (Help Center, ©2026).**
  - Disclosure is required for realistic altered or synthetic content: making a real person appear to say or do something they didn't, altering footage of real events or places, or showing a realistic scene that didn't occur.
  - Not required: clearly unrealistic or fully animated content (YouTube's examples include an AI-generated missile animation in a fully animated video), minor aesthetic edits, and production help such as AI scripts, titles, thumbnails, or captions. Cloning your own voice for voiceovers is listed as minor.
  - For non-photorealistic or animated AI content, any label appears in the expanded description, not the player. YouTube may auto-label based on C2PA metadata or its own detection.
  - YouTube's wording: "Disclosing AI content alone won't limit a video's audience or impact its eligibility to earn money." Repeatedly failing to disclose required content can lead to labels, removal, or YPP suspension.
  - [HIGH] — [YouTube Help, altered or synthetic content](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491); [YouTube Blog, 2024 disclosure launch](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/disclosing-ai-generated-content/)
- **TikTok.**
  - A label is required for AI-generated or significantly edited content that depicts realistic people or scenes. Cartoon, anime, or painterly styles generally don't require it, though TikTok encourages disclosure for other fully generated content.
  - TikTok auto-labels via C2PA Content Credentials (announced 2024, integrated about Jan 2025 per secondary sources). Auto labels reportedly can't be removed.
  - TikTok reportedly says enabling the AI-generated label does not affect distribution if the video follows Community Guidelines.
  - I could not load TikTok's own support page (it redirected and returned no content), so these points come from secondary sources. [MED-LOW] — [Hootsuite, TikTok AI disclosure](https://blog.hootsuite.com/ai-disclosure-policy-for-tiktok/); [Kabo guide](https://kabo.sh/guides/tiktok-ai-content-label); [Storrito](https://storrito.com/resources/tiktoks-2026-ai-labeling-rules-and-what-they-signal-for-platform-governance/)
- **Meta (Instagram/Facebook).**
  - Meta replaced "Made with AI" with "AI info" in 2024. The label is applied when creators self-disclose or Meta detects industry signals such as C2PA metadata.
  - Self-disclosure is expected for realistic AI video or audio. One guide suggests AI-generated voices should be labeled, but I found no primary Meta page confirming the voice-specific rule.
  - In 2026 Instagram began testing an optional profile-level "AI creator" label.
  - [LOW-MED, secondary] — [Spectrum News 2024 on Meta labeling](https://spectrumnews1.com/ca/la/technology/2024/04/05/meta-rolls-out-new-labeling-system-for-ai-generated-content); [Gradually.ai guide](https://www.gradually.ai/en/instagram-ai-label/); [Engadget on AI creator label test](https://engadget.com/2162426/instagram-is-testing-optional-ai-creator-labels); [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/414862/instagram-unveils-new-ai-creator-account-label.html)

### Inferences
- **Pacing target.** About 150-170 wpm (2.5-2.8 words per second) for the owl narrator: a 30 s video is about 75-85 words, and a 60 s video about 150-170 words. Allow up to about 180 wpm in hook or montage bursts, but never sustain more than about 180, because word-by-word captions must stay readable (BBC ceiling) and non-native viewers need headroom. Slow to about 130-140 wpm for the one key "aha" sentence.
- **Pauses.** Use 0.3-0.6 s beats after the hook question, before the reveal or number, and before the payoff line. Fill pauses with a visual or SFX rather than dead air. [inference, untested]
- **Energy and style.** Give the owl conversational "smart friend" energy, not announcer-style. Use the young guy as the audience surrogate who asks the dumb-but-real question; his lines can be faster and more emotional, creating contrast that resets attention. [inference]
- **AI voice.** Perception research says listeners mostly can't tell, but stated trust drops when they know. A clearly cartoon character voice (an owl) frames the AI voice as character performance, not a fake human. Do not clone a real person's voice; that triggers disclosure on YouTube and likely on Meta and TikTok.
- **Labeling.** For a fully animated 2D cartoon with a generic, non-cloned AI voice, the conservative policy is:
  - YouTube: not required; if added, it appears only in the description, with no reach penalty per YouTube.
  - TikTok: not required for cartoon style. Optional disclosure reportedly has no distribution effect.
  - Instagram: likely not required. Watch for auto-applied "AI info" from C2PA metadata embedded by AI tools (image or voice generators). A transparent bio line such as "animated, AI-voiced owl" preempts trust backlash.
  - Never depict real, identifiable people (politicians, celebrities) with AI. That moves content into realistic or "real person" territory.

### Gaps
- No study measures AI vs human narration effects on short-form watch time.
- No primary Meta document confirming whether an AI voice on an animated Reel needs "AI info".
- TikTok's own AI label support page could not be fetched.

## 4. Music and sound effects: trending vs original audio, volume relative to voice, and the role of SFX

### Takeaway
Hard evidence is thin. The strongest supported points: TikTok's own research says sound matters to its users, and Instagram's ranking favors original content (2022 and 2024 changes). For voice-led explainers, keep music as a low, ducked bed (about 15-25 dB under the voice during speech) and master to about -14 LUFS integrated. Trending audio matters less for voice-led explainers than for music-led trends.

### Cited Findings
- TikTok/Kantar (2021): 88% say sound is essential to TikTok, and 73% stop and look at ads with audio. [MED, vendor] — [TikTok for Business](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/kantar-report-how-brands-are-making-noise-and-driving-impact-with-sound-on-tiktok)
- Instagram has favored original content over reposts since 2022, and in 2024 Mosseri announced reposted aggregator content would be replaced by or labeled to the original. A 2026 report says watermarks, minor crops, or basic reposts don't qualify as original. [MED] — [Engadget 2024](https://www.engadget.com/instagram-ranking-algorithm-original-content-200028046.html); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2303663); [PetaPixel Apr 2026](https://petapixel.com/2026/04/30/new-instagram-policies-target-reposted-content/)
- Music under narration: guides recommend ducking music 15-20 dB, 18-24 dB, or 18-25 dB below the voice during speech. One source suggests far lower (-30 to -35 dB). No authoritative standard exists. [LOW, consensus] — [Overseer OS](https://www.overseeros.com/blog/background-music-volume-under-voiceover); [Choppity help](https://help.choppity.com/articles/0057970-background-music-sfx-and-volume-mixing); [ShortsFast](https://shortsfast.com/blog/mix-background-music-under-narration-without-masking-speech/); [Tempolor](https://www.tempolor.com/blog/?p=684)
- Loudness: master to about -14 LUFS integrated with true peak under -1 dBTP. TikTok and Reels reportedly normalize near -14 LUFS like YouTube. Check on phone speakers and on headphones. [LOW-MED] — [IRPR Sound, mixing for YouTube](https://sounddesign.irpr.agency/guides/how-to-mix-audio-for-youtube/); [OpenClip LUFS](https://openclip.app/learn/lufs.md)

### Inferences
- **Original vs trending audio.** For a voice-narrated explainer, use original audio: the VO plus a licensed or library bed. A trending song competes with the narrator and adds little discovery value when the VO dominates. A low trending sound bed is only worth it if the platform library offers it and it's inaudible under speech, and on Instagram/TikTok commercial-music licensing rules still apply. [inference; no comparative data found]
- **Volume.** VO about -14 LUFS integrated. Music bed ducked about 18-22 dB under the VO while speaking, rising in gaps. SFX (whoosh on scene change, pop on caption or number reveal, "cha-ching" for money, gulp or heartbeat for body topics) peak about 6-10 dB under the VO and never collide with key words. [inference from the mixing guides]
- **SFX role.** SFX mark each visual change and reveal, giving sound-on viewers micro-rewards. Because the visuals are cartoon, Foley-style comedic SFX also carry humor. No study found quantifying SFX effects on retention.

### Gaps
- No credible study comparing trending vs original audio on reach or retention for explainers.
- No study quantifying SFX effects on retention in short-form.

## 5. Writing for sends, saves, and comments without engagement bait, and what each platform penalizes

### Takeaway
On Instagram, Mosseri (Jan 2025) named watch time, likes per reach, and sends per reach as top ranking signals. Sends matter slightly more for reaching non-followers (unconnected reach). The often-cited "sends are worth 3-5x likes" multiplier is unsourced. Meta's distribution guidelines demote engagement bait ("comment YES", "tag 10 friends", vote/react/share bait). TikTok makes engagement-bait and spammy content ineligible for the For You feed. For an explainer, the strongest send triggers are "this is so you or us" relatability (the young guy's money or body mistakes), useful surprises a viewer wants to pass on ("did you know your…"), and specific-person sharing ("send to the friend who…"), phrased as natural lines, not commands.

### Cited Findings
- **Instagram signals.** In January 2025 Mosseri pointed creators to watch time, likes, and sends, calling sends per reach "one of the most important signals" in ranking. Likes are slightly more important for connected content (followers) and sends slightly more for unconnected content. Mosseri reportedly said comments count but are not most important and can correlate with negativity; that quote is secondary and I did not find the original video. [MED] — [Social Media Today, Instagram algorithm insights 2025](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-algorithm-insights-2025/738034/); [Search Engine Journal, sends matter more](https://searchenginejournal.com/instagram-algorithm-shift-why-sends-matter-more-than-ever/521389); [Social Samosa](https://www.socialsamosa.com/news-2/instagram-reveals-key-factors-for-boosting-reach-8646916)
- **Multiplier claim.** The "sends drive 3-5x more reach than likes" claim circulates without a named source, and no Meta-published coefficient exists. [LOW, flag as unverified] — [Creator Lane](https://creatorlanehq.com/blog/instagram-sends-per-reach-2026); [SocialDay](https://socialday.live/features/instagrams-dm-share-signal-now-drives-3-to-5-times-more-reach-than-likes)
- **Meta engagement bait.** Meta's Transparency Center lists engagement bait among content-distribution guideline violations that get reduced distribution. Commonly cited examples: "comment YES if you agree" and "tag ten friends". I could not load the page text itself; see the gap below. [MED, policy exists; details via secondary] — [Meta Transparency Center, engagement bait](https://transparency.meta.com/features/approach-to-ranking/content-distribution-guidelines/engagement-bait/); [Influencer Marketing Hub, sends-per-reach playbook](https://influencermarketinghub.com/instagram-sends-per-reach-playbook/)
- **TikTok.**
  - Content can stay up but be "FYF ineligible". TikTok states, "We make ineligible for the FYF certain content that may not be suitable for a broad audience," and that low views don't necessarily mean a rule was broken. [HIGH] — [TikTok Community Guidelines, FYF standards](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/fyf-standards)
  - Third-party summaries say spammy or deceptive behavior, including engagement-bait patterns ("like for like", fake rewards for follows, misleading claims to inflate views), and unoriginal or duplicated content are FYF-ineligible. [LOW-MED] — [Sirency, how the TikTok algorithm works](https://www.sirency.com/blog/how-the-tiktok-algorithm-works); [TTCalculator](https://ttcalculator.net/learn/get-on-fyp-consistently/)
  - No per-action point system (for example "share = 5 points") has been confirmed by TikTok. [LOW-MED] — [TTCalculator](https://ttcalculator.net/learn/get-on-fyp-consistently/)
- **Instagram originality.** Original content is favored, and aggregator reposts are demoted or replaced. This is a reason for the page to post native files, not TikTok-watermarked exports. [MED] — [Engadget 2024](https://www.engadget.com/instagram-ranking-algorithm-original-content-200028046.html)

### Inferences
Scripting rules for this page (practitioner-derived; untested):
- **Sends ("this is so you").**
  - Build each video around a mistake or misconception the young guy embodies ("I thought my tax refund was free money"), so viewers recognize a friend.
  - End on a line that names a person type naturally: "If your friend still keeps savings in checking… yeah." This is a statement, not "send this to them!"
  - For the body topic, use "wait, WHAT?" facts people forward ("your stomach gets a new lining every few days"), with the fact on screen as a shareable frame.
- **Saves (reference value).**
  - Include one "screenshot-able" frame: a 3-step checklist, a number rule ("50/30/20"), or a labeled diagram held for at least 1.5-2 s near the end.
  - Script a natural cue: "Here's the cheat sheet," not "SAVE THIS."
  - No source quantifying save drivers was found; this is inference.
- **Comments without bait.**
  - Ask a genuine, specific question tied to the content that has many valid answers: "What's the dumbest money myth you believed?" or "Which body fact do you want next?"
  - Or end on a mild, defensible stance that invites debate ("Renting isn't throwing money away. Fight me, guy."), voiced by a character so it reads as story, not provocation.
  - Avoid "comment YES", "type 1 if…", "tag 3 friends", "like if you agree", and "share or bad luck". These match Meta's engagement-bait categories and TikTok's spam patterns.
- **Controversy.** Light, factual "myth-busting" controversy drives comments. Avoid partisan political, financial-advice, or medical-advice framing: it's a brand risk, and TikTok's FYF standards flag overgeneralized health advice.

## 6. Accessibility and clarity for non-native English viewers

### Takeaway
Research supports same-language captions improving comprehension for non-native viewers, most clearly at low-to-intermediate proficiency, with mixed results in the broader literature. Full or key-phrase captions help most, and well-designed animation can carry part of the load. Combine plain vocabulary, about 150-160 wpm speech, phrase-segmented captions, and visuals that literally illustrate key terms.

### Cited Findings
- **Proficiency effect.** In a study of 80 Italian EFL students watching an English lecture, comprehension was modulated by proficiency. The study compared human-corrected captions, automatic captions, and no captions. The authors describe prior literature as mixed: captions are sometimes beneficial, sometimes neutral, sometimes detrimental. [HIGH] — [Ca' Foscari / IRIS paper](https://iris.unive.it/retrieve/a5681e93-89f2-46d9-9942-dcfc36fec28f/Towards_a_More_Inclusive_Learning_Environment_The_.pdf)
- **Caption type.** A study using MIT OpenCourseWare lectures found that full-text captions and key-sentence/phrase captions helped participants' understanding most. [MED] — [APSCE/ICCE paper](https://library.apsce.net/index.php/ICCE/article/download/2195/2071/3159)
- **Animation interaction.** A 2023 *ReCALL* study (Cambridge) compared full vs partial captions with animated vs static visuals. The gain from full captions was less pronounced with animated video content, suggesting visuals share the comprehension load. [HIGH] — [ReCALL / Cambridge Core PDF](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/2F0479F52D67232CF84DF5048482F98D/S0958344022000088a.pdf/efficacy-of-different-presentation-modes-for-l2-video-comprehension-full-versus-partial-display-of-verbal-and-nonverbal-input.pdf)
- **Smaller studies.** Two small studies (21 Chinese-caption learners, 2010; 12 Saudi English learners, qualitative) report captions aiding comprehension. [LOW, small n] — [IACIS 2010](https://iacis.org/iis/2010/219-224_LV2010_1550.pdf); [Alkhatnai, ICT4LL 2010](https://conference.pixel-online.net/conferences/ICT4LL2010/common/download/Proceedings_pdf/ILT13-Alkhatnai.pdf)
- **Comprehension ceiling.** Comprehension holds until about 2x normal speech (about 275 wpm) for lecture learners, mostly native-level. Non-native thresholds are likely lower (inference). [HIGH, adjacent] — [UCLA](https://newsroom.ucla.edu/releases/learning-while-speed-watching-class-videos)

### Inferences
- Plain-language rules:
  - Common words, defined once ("interest, the rent you pay on money").
  - One idea per sentence, sentences of 12 words or fewer.
  - Avoid idioms and US-only slang in the owl's lines; the young guy may use light slang, but the caption should still read clearly.
  - Spell out numbers as numerals on screen ("$1,000", "37%").
  - Avoid homophone-heavy puns that only work in audio.
- Captions must be accurate (hand-check auto-captions), because the proficiency study distinguished human-corrected from auto captions. For a US audience the main beneficiaries are muted viewers, Deaf and hard-of-hearing viewers, and ESL immigrants.
- Animation reinforces vocabulary: when the owl says "compound interest", show the visual (a snowball growing) at the same moment. The ReCALL finding suggests good visuals let captions stay lean.
- Contrast and readability: white text with a black stroke, plus a high-contrast highlight color (yellow or green on dark outline). Do not rely on color alone to convey meaning. Avoid fast flicker or strobe transitions. [standard accessibility practice; no specific source fetched in this pass]
